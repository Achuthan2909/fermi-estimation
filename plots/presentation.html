What is a Fermi Estimation?

Named after physicist Enrico Fermi — a back-of-the-envelope calculation designed to estimate the order of magnitude (10×) of a complex or seemingly impossible quantity. Not to find the perfect answer, but to reach a reasonable approximation that informs decision-making.

🏭 Engineering

  • Checking if a design is physically plausible before deep simulation
  • Rapid feasibility checks

💼 Business

  • "How many gas stations are in Chicago?"
  • Market sizing in interviews

🔬 Science

  • Validating experimental results against physical constants
  • Sanity-checking simulations

Why Do LLMs Struggle?

📉 The Drift Problem

If an LLM is off by one order of magnitude on three sub-steps, the final answer is off by 10³. LLMs lack a sanity-check mechanism to detect when their output has drifted into physical impossibility.

🧱 No Physical Intuition

LLMs know the statistical relationship between tokens like "piano" and "size" — they don't feel how big a piano is. There is no grounded physical sense of scale.

🎯 False Precision

LLMs are trained to be "helpful" and "precise" — so they produce specific numbers rather than rounded estimates, masking the uncertainty of underlying guesses.

🗃️ Data Contamination

The model may have already seen the exact Fermi question during pretraining — giving a correct answer from memorisation rather than actual reasoning. We removed 180 synthetic questions to test this.

Model

Output Format

Structured JSON with factors, formula, calculation, and a final OOM integer — machine-parseable and human-auditable.

Evaluation Design

📊 Dataset

  • 5,008 total questions
  • Sources: Science Olympiad, FermiP, synthetic
  • Train / Val / Eval splits
  • 180 synthFP questions filtered for clean eval

📐 Scoring (FP Score)

  • score = max(0, 1 − δ / 3)
  • δ = |log₁₀(pred) − gold OOM|
  • 1.0 = exact, 0.0 at ±3 decades
  • Rule of Five for OOM rounding

📋 Prompt Contract

  • Single parseable JSON output
  • Fields: factors, formula, calculation, final OOM
  • Machine-parseable for scoring
  • Human-auditable for transparency
Structured JSON output enforces auditability:

{ "factors": [...], "formula": "N × m × 365", "calculation": "6e9 × 70 × 365 = 1.5e14",
  "final": { "value": 1.5e14, "unit": "kg·days", "oom": 14 },
  "confidence": "medium" }

SFT Training Dynamics

Training loss (blue) decreases steadily over ~270 steps. Red dots show val set mean score at checkpoints 20, 140, and final — rising from 0.594 → 0.617 → 0.691, confirming the model generalises as training progresses.

Performance by OOM Range

Both models perform best on everyday-scale questions (−3 to 9). Performance degrades sharply at extremes — Very High OOM (≥20) has a 48.5% zero-score rate for RL v2.

Error rate rises sharply outside the everyday range for both models. RL v2 reduces error rate in the mid range but shows minimal improvement at extreme OOMs due to the zero-gradient problem in GRPO.

Score Distribution & Confidence Calibration

Bimodal distribution: a spike at score=0 (complete failures) and a spread across 0.3–0.9. RL v2 shifts mass from the 0 bin into the mid-range (+6.6% parse_ok).

High-confidence answers are wrong ~26% of the time — nearly identical to medium confidence. The model does not reliably know when it doesn't know.

Science Olympiad (scioly) questions perform well in the everyday range but poorly at extremes. Real FermiP (realFP) questions show more uniform performance. All sources struggle with Very High (≥20) and Very Low (≤−10) OOM questions.

RL Reward Function (v2)

Formula

reward = 0.85 × oom_score + 0.15 × struct_score

oom_score = max(0, 1 − δ / 1.5)
δ = |log₁₀(pred) − gold_oom|

Tail OOM ×1.3 if gold_oom ≤ −4 or ≥ 10

parse fail → −1.0
schema fail → −0.5

Design Choices

  • Divisor 1.5 (not 3.0) for steeper gradient
  • 85/15 split: accuracy over structure
  • Tail OOM ×1.3 multiplier to amplify signal on hard questions
  • Hard −1.0 penalty for parse fails keeps JSON quality high

Conclusions

✅ What Worked

  • SFT significantly improved structured output quality (+6.6% parse_ok)
  • RL (GRPO) pushed beyond imitation — +7.9% within-1-decade on clean eval
  • SynthFP removal revealed true fine-tuning gains (+0.058 vs +0.008)
  • Fact-grounded verification pipeline produced higher-quality training data
  • Log-space reward prevented overflow on extreme OOM values

❌ What Didn't

  • Tail OOM performance remains poor (48.5% zero-score for ≥20)
  • Confidence is uncalibrated — model is equally wrong at high/medium confidence
  • RL v3 with tail OOM oversampling + group_size=4 caused reward decline (zero-gradient problem)
  • Model can be confidently wrong on knowledge-gap questions (e.g. Sudoku count)

🔭 Future Directions

  • Increase GRPO group_size to 8 to fix zero-gradient problem on hard questions
  • Target tail OOM oversampling with larger group size
  • Add confidence calibration penalty to reward function
  • SFT pass specifically on tail OOM questions with verified traces
  • Per-domain performance analysis to identify weakest question types
  • Multi-step verification: model checks own answer before committing
